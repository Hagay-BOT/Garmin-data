# Running Economy — Form, Cadence, Efficiency

## Cadence
- Optimal range: 170–180 steps per minute (spm) for most recreational runners
- Below 160 spm: likely over-striding, higher impact forces, increased injury risk
- At 165–170: acceptable, improvement target
- Above 180 spm: generally efficient — don't force higher
- Cadence improvements reduce injury risk: tibial stress fractures, IT band syndrome, patellofemoral pain
- **How to improve**: run at 170+ spm regardless of pace; use a metronome; short quick steps > long bounding strides

## Ground Contact Time (GCT)
- Elite runners: 160–200 ms
- Good recreational runners: 220–260 ms
- Beginners: 260–300+ ms
- Lower GCT = more elastic energy return, less braking force
- Garmin data field: `gct_ms`

## Stride Length
- Optimal stride length is a result of cadence and pace, not a direct target
- Over-striding = foot lands ahead of center of mass = braking force = injury risk
- Key cue: foot should land under (or slightly behind) hip
- Garmin data field: `stride_length_m`

## Vertical Oscillation
- Good runners: 6–8 cm vertical bounce
- Excessive bounce: > 9 cm (wasted energy going up instead of forward)
- Garmin data field: `vertical_oscillation_cm`

## Vertical Ratio
- Formula: Vertical Oscillation / Stride Length × 100
- Good: < 8%
- Excellent: < 7%
- Garmin data field: `vertical_ratio_pct`

## Cardiac Drift
- Definition: rise in HR during sustained Z2 effort at constant pace
- Measured as: average HR in last third of run minus average HR in first third
- Acceptable drift: < 5 bpm (well-trained aerobic base)
- Moderate drift: 5–10 bpm (needs Z2 work)
- Poor drift: > 10 bpm (dehydrated, fatigued, or undertrained aerobically)
- **Key insight**: if drift > 10 bpm in a supposed "easy" run, the effort was too hard OR aerobic base is underdeveloped

## Running Economy Improvements
1. More Z2 volume → improved fat oxidation → lower HR at same pace
2. Strength work (calf raises, hip flexors, glutes) → better power transfer
3. Plyometrics → improved elastic energy storage in tendons
4. Cadence drills → reduced ground contact time
