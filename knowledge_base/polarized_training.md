# Polarized Training — Seiler 80/20 Model

## Core Principle
The 80/20 Polarized Training Model (Stephen Seiler, 2010) divides training intensity:
- **80% of sessions**: Zone 1–2 (easy, aerobic base, conversational pace)
- **20% of sessions**: Zone 4–5 (hard, threshold and above)
- **Zone 3 should be minimized** — "no man's land" — not easy enough for recovery, not hard enough for adaptation

## Why Polarized Works
- Most amateur runners train too much in Z3 (moderate effort) — "junk miles"
- Z2 builds aerobic base: mitochondrial density, fat oxidation, cardiac stroke volume
- Z4-Z5 creates the adaptation stimulus: VO2max, lactate tolerance, neuromuscular power
- Z3 accumulates fatigue without the adaptation benefit of Z4-Z5

## Zone 2 — The Aerobic Base
Based on San Millán & Brooks (2018) and Seiler's recommendations:
- Target: 60–70% MaxHR (Zone 2 by %MaxHR definition)
- Feel: "could hold a full conversation"
- Lactate level: 1.0–2.0 mmol/L (aerobic threshold, not measured here)
- Adaptation timeline: consistent Z2 base shows results in 4–12 weeks
- Minimum effective dose: 3–4 hours/week of Z2

## Quality Sessions (Z4-Z5)
- 1–2 per week maximum for recreational/amateur runners
- Typical formats: intervals (800m–2000m), tempo runs, fartlek
- Recovery: 48h minimum between quality sessions
- Never do 2 hard sessions back-to-back

## Zone Distribution Targets (weekly, by time in zones)
- Z1: 10–20%
- Z2: 60–70%
- Z3: < 10%
- Z4: 10–15%
- Z5: 2–5%

## Warning Signs of Too Much Z3
- Average HR during "easy" runs > 75% MaxHR
- Cardiac drift > 10 bpm in Z2 runs (poor aerobic efficiency)
- Constant fatigue without performance improvement
